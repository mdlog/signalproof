2 / 11

Anyone can claim coverage numbers.
Nobody can prove them.

Operators publish coverage maps. DePIN networks pay contributors for connectivity data. Venues and public programs buy both. None of them can audit what they are paying for.

The data is self-reported. A coverage claim is a number someone typed. The device that produced it, the place, the time — none of it is checkable after the fact.

The incentive is to inflate. Where contributors are paid per sample, a fake sample is worth exactly as much as a real one until someone can tell them apart.

Location data is the price of honesty today. The usual fix is to collect precise GPS trails, which no one should have to hand over to prove their phone had two bars.

What SignalProof changes

The measurement becomes an event on a public chain, the event is proven to a second chain by the Attestcoin Protocol, and a contract there decides — from the proof alone — whether it is real enough to pay for.

Not the gateway. Not the relayer. Not the person who ran the test. The proof.

What the precompile proves, and the two things it does not.

SignalProofSettlement inherits ASCBase and overrides one hook. The BlockProver precompile proves inclusion in an attested Sepolia block — not that the transaction succeeded, and not which contract emitted its logs.

Protocol detailWhat we do
Receipt statusReject unless receiptStatus == 1. Inclusion is not success.
Emitter bindingReject a log whose emitter is not our registry. Missing from the docs' SimpleMinterASC example, present in Gluwa's ASCLoanManager; mirrored here with a whole-system test where a forged registry holds a valid proof.
chainKeyResolved at startup from getSupportedChains() on chainId. It is a uint64; chainName is hex-encoded; key 1 means another chain on Mainnet.
Attestation waitPoll getLatestAttestedHeightAndHash once per tick. waitUntilHeightAttested blocks 8–20 minutes and would freeze the queue.
getProofCheck .success, unwrap .data, 120 s timeout with our own backoff.
Two replay layers

ASCBase deduplicates by queryId; the contract also refuses a measurementRoot it has already settled.

Batch route

Many measurements, one continuity proof, one transaction. A bad entry is skipped and reported, never fatal to the honest ones beside it.

Pull-payment rewards

No value moves on the verification path, so a reverting receiver cannot block settlement for anyone else.

6 / 11

Three ways to get paid for nothing, found and closed before submission.

Each exploit is kept in the test suite with its assertions inverted, so reopening the hole fails a test instead of quietly working.

1
The registry was permissionless
Anyone could call the real registry, name themselves as payee, obtain a genuine proof and settle it. Emitter binding passes, because the event really came from the bound registry. Fixed by gating admission to the relayer that carries the gateway's checks. Redeployed. AdvUnlimitedMint.t.sol
2
One measurement paid twice
Two settlement routes, one registry, two settled maps. The same genuine proof could be paid from both pools. The batch contract now defers to any root a sibling already paid, via a guarded staticcall that degrades to no cross-check rather than refusing everyone. DoubleSettlement.t.sol
3
One poisoned log killed a batch
A Sepolia transaction that touched the registry and emitted a lookalike log made the batch revert forever, taking the honest measurements down with it. Foreign logs are now skipped and reported through ForeignLogSkipped. BatchPoison.t.sol

Why this matters for Attestcoin depth. The precompile was never wrong in any of these. Every exploit used a genuine proof. The application layer is where cross-chain security is won or lost, and that is the layer this project spent its time on.

Batching is cheaper per entry, and it gets more expensive as it grows.

Two live runs are not enough to state a saving, so BatchGasProbe.t.sol holds the proof shape fixed and varies only the batch size.

A marginal entry costs 45–71k gas against 78k for a batch of one — and the number rises with size, because the decoder allocates memory it never reclaims and the quadratic memory-expansion term takes over.

So the worker caps a batch at 50, not at the block ceiling. The probe puts that ceiling at 600 entries for a 75,000,000-gas CC3 block; 800 does not fit.

Live numbers agree in shape: 253,284 and 423,360 gas for three measurements, against 150,640 for one. The spread comes from the proof the attestation service happens to return, not from the batch.

9 / 11

Coarse by construction. Measured, not asked.

Location never leaves the phone

The area is a geohash of at most six characters, enforced by the gateway, stored on-chain as bytes32. A cell is roughly 1.2 km by 0.6 km; a contributor is somewhere inside the box, never at a dot. The raw coordinate is converted in the same function that reads it and is never transmitted.

The wallet is an identity, not a signer

The relayer pays gas on both chains. The connected address only decides who accrues the reward. The one transaction a contributor ever sends is claim().

What is not done, stated before a judge finds it.

Known limitations
  • Payload signatures are stored and structurally checked, not yet cryptographically verified.
  • Read direction only, Sepolia to Creditcoin. Write-ability has no public reference implementation and has not cleared third-party audit.
  • Attested is not finalized: attestation runs 65–85 blocks ahead of Sepolia's finalized tag. A deep reorg could in principle invalidate an attested height.
  • End to end is 9–13 minutes. A live real-time demo is not possible; the demo pre-warms a proof.
  • No anti-Sybil scoring or platform attestation yet; the browser client is the measurement client.
Next 90 days
  • Verify contributor signatures on the gateway and bind them to the on-chain sessionHash.
  • Buyer API: area coverage snapshots with retention policy and authentication.
  • Native measurement client with background sampling and device attestation.
  • Sybil resistance: per-device rate limits, stake-weighted rewards, cell-level anomaly detection.
  • Write-ability once an audited reference implementation exists, so settlement can push results back to the source chain.

11 / 11

Why this belongs on Creditcoin.

The buyer is real. Operators planning capacity, venues negotiating with carriers, and public infrastructure programs auditing coverage commitments all pay for connectivity data today, and none of it is verifiable.

The proof is the product. A coverage snapshot whose every sample can be traced to an attested Sepolia event and a Creditcoin settlement is a different good from a spreadsheet of claims.

Creditcoin is where it settles. Rewards accrue in CTC, the settlement contract is the source of truth, and the Attestcoin Protocol is the reason no oracle operator sits in the middle.
